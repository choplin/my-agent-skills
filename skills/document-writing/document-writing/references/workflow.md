# Editorial workflow

Use this reference to select stages and checkpoints. It combines established
editorial, technical-writing, and academic-writing practice; it is not a claim
that every document follows one universal outline.

## Why the passes are ordered this way

Structural or developmental editing works on the organization and content of a
document for its audience, medium, and purpose. Stylistic editing works on flow
and coherence at paragraph and sentence scale. Copyediting handles correctness
and consistency. Proofreading checks the final rendered object. Work from large
scale to small scale because a structural change invalidates polished sentences
more often than a copyedit invalidates structure.

Primary references:

- Editors Canada, [Professional Editorial Standards](https://editors.ca/publications/professional-editorial-standards/)
- Editors Canada, [The Fundamentals of Editing](https://editors.ca/publications/professional-editorial-standards/fundamentals-editing/)
- CIEP, [About proofreading and editing](https://www.ciep.uk/resource/about-proofreading-and-editing.html)
- CIEP, [What is an editorial brief?](https://www.ciep.uk/resource/what-is-an-editorial-brief-and-how-does-it-help-both-authors-and-editorial-professionals.html)

## Common stages

| Stage | Main question | Typical artifact | Exit condition |
|---|---|---|---|
| Assignment | For whom, for what use, under what constraints? | brief | Success and intervention scope are explicit. |
| Discovery | What does the material establish, suggest, or lack? | source notes | Claims, evidence, gaps, and provenance are inspectable. |
| Focus | What is this document really about? | focus note | Governing question and controlling idea are specific. |
| Content model | What conceptual and evidential pieces are needed? | content model | Centrality, novelty, relationships, and support are visible. |
| Plot | How will the reader move through those pieces? | genre-specific plot | The sequence delivers the governing idea. |
| Draft | What is the complete first realization? | draft | Every plotted movement has usable prose or representation. |
| Developmental edit | Is the substance and architecture right? | revised plot and draft | Content, order, proportion, and emphasis work as a whole. |
| Line edit | Does the prose carry the intended movement? | revised draft | Paragraph and sentence realization is natural and coherent. |
| Copyedit | Is the text locally correct and consistent? | copyedited draft | Terminology, references, grammar, syntax, and style pass. |
| Proof/accept | Does the delivered object fulfill the assignment? | final document | Content and rendering are complete and usable. |

Small documents may combine artifacts, for example `brief-focus` or
`content-model-plot`. Combining is not skipping: the combined artifact must
still preserve the relevant decisions.

## Existing-document branch

Do not assume that an existing table of contents expresses the intended logic.
After establishing the editorial assignment, make a reverse outline from the
actual text. Use it to diagnose:

- the job each passage currently performs;
- the claim and support it contains;
- its relation to the governing idea;
- repetition, disproportion, missing premises, and unprepared transitions;
- concepts defined because they are easy to define rather than important.

Then create a revised plot before substantive revision. This keeps diagnosis
separate from the proposed solution.

Purdue OWL's [reverse outlining guidance](https://owl.purdue.edu/owl/graduate_writing/introduction_to_writing/documents/drafting-your-document/handouts/genre-analysis-activity.pdf)
is a useful basis for recording each paragraph's main idea and relation to the
whole.

## Technical-document route

Start with the reader's task and prior knowledge. Google Technical Writing
frames useful content as what the audience needs minus what it already knows,
and recommends defining scope and a high-level outline before drafting:

- [Audience](https://developers.google.com/tech-writing/one/audience)
- [Documents](https://developers.google.com/tech-writing/one/documents)
- [Organizing large documents](https://developers.google.com/tech-writing/two/large-docs)

Choose the document kind from the reader need, not from an empty folder
structure. Diátaxis distinguishes tutorial, how-to, reference, and explanation;
use the distinction as a compass rather than forcing every subject into four
containers: [Diátaxis in five minutes](https://diataxis.fr/start-here/) and
[Diátaxis as a guide to work](https://www.diataxis.fr/how-to-use-diataxis/).

## Academic route

Record the research question, gap, contribution, claims, evidence, method,
limitations, target venue, and applicable reporting guideline before selecting
a section pattern. IMRAD is normal for many original research articles but is
not a universal academic outline. Reviews, theoretical papers, case reports,
and other genres require different structures.

- ICMJE, [Preparing a Manuscript for Submission](https://icmje.org/recommendations/browse/manuscript-preparation/preparing-for-submission.html)
- EQUATOR Network, [Reporting guidelines](https://www.equator-network.org/)

Journal instructions and study-type reporting standards are constraints on the
plot. They do not determine the paper's intellectual contribution.

## Book and chapter route

Separate the book-level promise from the job of each chapter. A chapter needs
its own movement while changing the reader in a way the whole book requires.
For nonfiction, track the argument, examples, explanatory sequence, and echoes
across chapters. For narrative work, track causal movement, viewpoint,
revelation, stakes, and scene purpose. Use developmental editing before line
editing in either case.
