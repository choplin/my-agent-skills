# General document plot

Use whichever prompts expose the document's movement. Delete the rest.

## Governing axis

What question, problem, or tension holds the document together? What answer or
change should the reader reach? Why does that matter to this reader now?

## Reader movement

Describe the path in any useful form: section cards, a narrative paragraph, a
sketch, questions and answers, or an annotated heading list.

For each important movement, consider:

- What prompts this part?
- What can the reader already be expected to know?
- What claim, action, example, or distinction does this part contribute?
- What changes for the reader before the next part?
- Why must it appear here?

## Concepts and evidence

Which concepts carry the argument? Which are familiar to the audience? Which
need context before naming or defining? What evidence or source supports each
material claim? What remains assumption or judgment?

## Shape and proportion

Where should the document slow down, turn, compare, demonstrate, or conclude?
What deserves space because it is important, and what is merely easy to explain?
Would any relationship be clearer as steps, a table, a diagram, or code?

## Open alternatives

Record plausible structures considered, unresolved choices, and why the current
path is preferred.

## Revision note

What changed in this revision, and why?
